\noindent This note accompanies ``Exact Stabilization and Local Decision Structure in Shortest-Bridge Repair Dynamics.'' It records the complementary N1/N2 atomic language and the finite short-chain compatibility theory. Notation and the Q2/Q3 mask-locality principle are as defined in the article.

\section{Full Atomic Language}

\begin{definition}[N1 action]\label{def:n1-action}
Let $Y$ be defective.  Consider every disconnected occupied Q2/Q3 mask of
$Y$ and every unoccupied voxel $v$ incident to at least one such mask.  The
singleton addition $\{v\}$ is an N1 action exactly when
\[
  \pwc(Y\cup\{v\}).
\]
Equivalently, the N1 generator forms the union of the missing positions in
all currently bad masks and retains precisely the one-voxel additions whose
successors pass the exact Q2/Q3 predicate.  If $Y$ is valid, no N1 action is
available.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Strict descent for the full atomic language]
\label{prop:full-atomic-descent}
Define
\[
 \Phi(Y)=|\Comp(Y)|+\mathbf 1[\operatorname{fail}(Y)].
\]
Every admissible N1 or N2 transition $Y\to Y'$ satisfies
$\Phi(Y')<\Phi(Y)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For an N1 action, the added voxel is drawn from a disconnected Q2/Q3 mask and
repairs that mask in a globally valid successor.  It is $6$-adjacent to an
occupied voxel of the current state, so the component count cannot increase,
while the failure indicator drops from one to zero.  For an N2 action, the
retained path joins two distinct components, reducing the component count by
at least one; the successor is valid by the N2 action definition in the article.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite Compatibility in Short Chain Families}

Consider the interaction between a minimum-cardinality baseline repair and a
second local repair in the family $\mathrm{chain}_L$.  The finite model has two
complementary descriptions.  Its minimum-cardinality repair family is
represented by walks on four repair columns, while support separation reduces
compatibility with the local repair to a fixed $3\times3\times3$ neighborhood.
Together these descriptions give a three-layer compatibility bound, attained
in the $L=5$ family.  The mathematical flow is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 B\in\mathcal M_0(\mathrm{chain}_L)
 &\xrightarrow{\text{walk representation}}\text{column walk},\\
 B&\xrightarrow{\text{localization}}B\cap\mathcal L(q)
   \xrightarrow{\text{three-layer span}}\pwc(Z_{B,q}).
\end{aligned}
\]

For a defective voxel set $F_0$, let
\[
\begin{aligned}
 m_0(F_0)&=\min\{\lvert R\rvert:\pwc(F_0\mathbin{\triangle}R)\},\\
 \mathcal M_0(F_0)&=
 \{R:\pwc(F_0\mathbin{\triangle}R),\\[-1mm]
 &\hspace{31mm}\lvert R\rvert=m_0(F_0)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Fix
\[
 \mathrm{Seed}=\{(-1,-1,-1),(-1,-1,0),(0,0,-1),(0,0,0)\}
\]
and, for $L\ge3$, put
\[
 \mathrm{chain}_L=\bigcup_{j=0}^{L-2}
   \bigl(\mathrm{Seed}+(0,0,j)\bigr).
\]
At each occupied $z$-layer the four repair columns are
$(-1,-1)$, $(0,0)$, $(-1,0)$, and $(0,-1)$; the two diagonally opposed pairs
are $(-1,-1)\leftrightarrow(0,0)$ and
$(-1,0)\leftrightarrow(0,-1)$.

A perturbation pair $(a,b)$ is called \emph{legitimate} in this finite setting
when it is isolated from $F_0$ in Chebyshev distance at least two, each single
perturbation preserves the complete minimal-repair family,
\[
 \mathcal M_0(F_0\cup\{a\})=
 \mathcal M_0(F_0\cup\{b\})=\mathcal M_0(F_0),
\]
and the pair creates a defect witness absent from $F_0$ and from both singly
perturbed states.  Thus legitimacy combines isolation, individual
invisibility, and joint visibility; equality of defect sets alone is not
sufficient.

For such a pair let $F_{a,b}=F_0\cup\{a,b\}$ and let $q$ be an associated
local repair voxel.  Given $B\in\mathcal M_0(F_0)$, set
\[
 Z_{B,q}=F_{a,b}\mathbin{\triangle}(B\cup\{q\}).
\]
Compatibility means $\pwc(Z_{B,q})$.  Its \emph{compatibility depth} is the
smallest number of consecutive decision layers whose choices determine this
verdict, with depth zero denoting a baseline-independent verdict.

\begin{proposition}[Walk representation of minimum repairs]
\label{prop:finite-walk-precursor}
For $L\in\{3,4,5\}$, the family
$\mathcal M_0(\mathrm{chain}_L)$ is in exact bijection with length-$L$ walks
on the four repair columns that never move between diagonally opposed columns
at consecutive layers.  Consequently,
\[
 \lvert\mathcal M_0(\mathrm{chain}_L)\rvert=4\cdot3^{L-1},
 \qquad N_L(r)=3^{L-r}
\]
for every admissible prefix of length $r\in\{1,\ldots,L-1\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $L=3$, complete enumeration establishes minimality, the repair family, and the
forbidden-diagonal transition rule.  For $L=4$,
exhaustive rejection of all smaller candidate repairs establishes $m_0=4$;
the completeness of the $108$-member size-four family is then checked by the
walk description and exact agreement with the finite reference classification.  For
$L=5$, exact equality and P-well-composedness validity are checked for the
$324$-member walk family against the finite reference classification; minimality and
completeness at this length use that complete finite classification rather
than a second enumeration of all smaller and size-five candidates.
Once the three finite bijections are established, the first layer has four
choices and each subsequent layer has three legal continuations, yielding
both formulas.
\end{proof}

To state the locality result, let $\mathcal N(v)$ be the set of edge and
vertex masks incident to a voxel $v$, let $C(h)$ be the voxels incident to a
mask $h$, and put
\[
 \mathcal L(q)=\bigcup_{h\in\mathcal N(q)}C(h)
              =q+\{-1,0,1\}^3,
 \qquad
 D_{\mathcal M}=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal M_0(F_0)}B.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Locality under support separation]
\label{lem:finite-precursor-localization}
Assume $q\notin D_{\mathcal M}$ and
\[
 C(h)\cap D_{\mathcal M}=\varnothing
 \quad\text{for every }h\in
 \bigl(\mathcal N(a)\cup\mathcal N(b)\bigr)\setminus\mathcal N(q).
\]
Then the verdict $\pwc(Z_{B,q})$ depends on
$B\in\mathcal M_0(F_0)$ only through $B\cap\mathcal L(q)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every $F_0\mathbin{\triangle}B$ is locally clean.  By
the mask-locality lemma in the article, toggling $a$, $b$, and $q$ can change only masks
in $\mathcal N(a)\cup\mathcal N(b)\cup\mathcal N(q)$.  On masks outside
$\mathcal N(q)$ the displayed disjointness condition excludes every voxel
whose occupancy can vary with $B$.  The remaining masks lie in
$\mathcal N(q)$, and their incident voxels have union $\mathcal L(q)$.
Therefore two baselines agreeing on $B\cap\mathcal L(q)$ give the same local
occupancies at every potentially affected mask and hence the same verdict.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Three-layer compatibility bound]
\label{cor:finite-precursor-depth}
Under the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-precursor-localization}, if every baseline repair selects
one decision voxel per layer, then compatibility depth is at most three.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $\mathcal L(q)=q+\{-1,0,1\}^3$, its intersection with any repair column
is contained in the three levels $q_z-1,q_z,q_z+1$.  Under the
one-decision-per-layer property, $B\cap\mathcal L(q)$ is therefore determined
by these three layer decisions.  Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-precursor-localization}
then shows that the same three decisions determine compatibility.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharpness and two compatibility mechanisms]
\label{prop:finite-precursor-sharpness}
For the distance-two candidate space of $\mathrm{chain}_5$, the exact quotient
has $76$ symmetry classes representing $528$ legitimate pairs.  Its depth
distribution contains four depth-three classes representing $12$ pairs, so
the bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-precursor-depth} is sharp in this
domain.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The finite classification enumerates all $528$ legitimate distance-two pairs
and partitions them into $76$ symmetry classes.  For each class
representative, the compatibility verdict is evaluated on the complete
$324$-member walk family from Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-walk-precursor}.
Comparing this truth table with the restrictions to consecutive decision
windows of lengths zero, one, two, and three gives the least determining
window, hence the compatibility depth.  The resulting class counts are
$64,6,2,4$ at depths $0,1,2,3$, while the corresponding orbit sizes sum to
$468,40,8,12$ pairs.  In particular, four classes representing $12$ pairs
require all three layers.  Corollary~\ref{cor:finite-precursor-depth} gives the
matching upper bound, so depth three is attained and the bound is sharp.
The complete orbit records are included in this Online Resource.
\end{proof}

The depth-three classes realize two genuinely different predicates on the
relevant decision window.  In the \emph{inclusion mechanism}, compatibility
requires every decision in the window to occupy the prescribed column
$(-1,-1)$, so the compatible subfamily is a prefix-restricted walk family.
In the \emph{exclusion/OR mechanism}, incompatibility is triggered whenever
the column $(-1,0)$ occurs at any position of the window; the predicate is
therefore a union of layerwise exclusions rather than a conjunction of
prescribed choices.

The $21$ support-separation checks are exactly the nonzero-depth applicability
instances: $3$ for $L=3$, $6$ for $L=4$, and $12$ for $L=5$.  Depth-zero
classes are excluded because their verdict is baseline-independent by
definition.  The complete orbit table, all representatives, the support-separation
checks, and the mechanism witnesses are included in this Online Resource.  The propositions above concern the stated finite
chain families.  The walk representation is established only for
$L\in\{3,4,5\}$, and support separation is not asserted to hold automatically
for arbitrary $L$.  These finite compatibility results concern only the stated short-chain
families and are not used in the proofs of the article's two main theorems.
