# C07-D6C — Exact-depth-3 atomic-planning counterexample

## 1. Verdict

Under the frozen C07-D5 atomic semantics, the universal depth-two claim is **REJECTED**.
There exists a finite state (X_3) such that

\[
d_{\mathrm{plan}}(X_3)=3.
\]

This is an exact finite counterexample: every atomic schedule of length at most two was enumerated and fails, while an explicit three-action schedule reaches `Goal`.

This result does **not** reject atomic completeness. The exhibited state is atomically reachable and has 409 shortest addition-set schedules of length three. Nor does it classify all states admitted by the D6B3 generator: the declared stop rule terminated the search at the first exact-depth-three hit.

## 2. Frozen semantics

The audit uses the D5 definitions without modification:

\[
A_{\mathrm{atom}}(X)=A_{N1}^{(1)}(X)\cup A_{N2}(X),
\]

where actions are identified by their addition sets and may carry multiple roles. Every successor recomputes defects, components, certificates, and action sets. The goal predicate is

\[
Goal(X)\iff \neg has\_fail(X)\ \land\ |\pi_0^{(6)}(X)|=1.
\]

`P_preserve` requires every genuine N2 component-pair certificate present before an action either to be resolved by that action or to remain bridge-repairable between its descendant components.

## 3. D6B3 family and search provenance

The generator took the three no-dual D6B2 orbit representatives and added exactly one voxel from the canonical (X_0) bounding box expanded by two lattice units. States were quotiented by translations and signed-axis permutations before admission.

The admission conditions were

\[
has\_fail(X),\qquad A_{N1}^{(1)}(X)\ne\varnothing,
\]

\[
A_{N1}^{(1)}(X)\cap A_{N2}(X)=\varnothing,
\]

and

\[
\{\Delta\in A_{\mathrm{atom}}(X):P_{\mathrm{preserve}}(X,\Delta)\}=\varnothing.
\]

The census was:

| Stage | Count |
|---|---:|
| Raw one-voxel seed extensions | 1,131 |
| Isometry-quotient states | 1,127 |
| Defective orbits | 1,124 |
| Orbits with a valid single N1 repair | 935 |

The first two tested admitted states had depth two. The third had exact depth three, triggering the predeclared stop rule. Accordingly, the 17 admitted states are not presented as a completed family classification.

For computational efficiency, rejected candidates were first screened using the compressed component-bridge decider. Every fully admitted state was then re-audited using the frozen compressed/full-monotone-order crosscheck. The decisive witness was additionally verified by a separate certificate script.

## 4. Counterexample state

The eight-voxel representative is

\[
\begin{aligned}
X_3=\{&(-4,1,0),(-3,-1,-1),(-1,-1,-1),(-1,0,-1),\\
      &(-1,0,0),(0,-1,-1),(0,-1,0),(0,2,-1)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Its four initial 6-components are

\[
\{(-4,1,0)\},\quad \{(-3,-1,-1)\},
\]

\[
\{(-1,-1,-1),(-1,0,-1),(-1,0,0),(0,-1,-1),(0,-1,0)\},
\]

and

\[
\{(0,2,-1)\}.
\]

The valid single N1 repairs are exactly

\[
A_{N1}^{(1)}(X_3)=\{(-1,-1,0),(0,0,0)\}.
\]

These are also the only initial atomic actions. Both are N1-only, so

\[
A_{N1}^{(1)}(X_3)\cap A_{N2}(X_3)=\varnothing.
\]

For each action, the strict certificate audit finds exactly one non-viable old N2 obligation: the pair comprising the isolated component \(\{(-4,1,0)\}\) and the five-voxel component. Hence neither action passes `P_preserve`.

## 5. Exact lower bound

Let (Succ^j(X_3)) denote states reached by exactly (j) frozen atomic actions, with action sets recomputed after every transition. Direct enumeration gives

\[
Goal\cap Succ^1(X_3)=\varnothing.
\]

There are two first actions. After choosing ((-1,-1,0)), 46 second actions are available; after choosing ((0,0,0)), 40 are available. All 86 length-two action sequences were evaluated, and

\[
Goal\cap Succ^2(X_3)=\varnothing.
\]

Therefore

\[
d_{\mathrm{plan}}(X_3)\ge 3.
\]

This lower bound is not inferred from `P_preserve`; it is certified by explicit enumeration of the complete frozen atomic action tree through depth two.

## 6. Explicit upper bound

One shortest plan is

\[
\Delta_1=\{(-1,-1,0)\}\qquad (N1\text{-only}),
\]

\[
\Delta_2=\{(-4,-1,-1),(-4,-1,0),(-4,0,0)\}\qquad (N2\text{-only}),
\]

\[
\Delta_3=\{(-4,1,-1),(-3,1,-1),(-2,1,-1),(-1,1,-1),(-1,2,-1)\}
\qquad (N2\text{-only}).
\]

Each addition set belongs to the recomputed atomic action set at its own state. The component-count trace is

\[
4\xrightarrow{\Delta_1}4\xrightarrow{\Delta_2}3\xrightarrow{\Delta_3}1,
\]

and the final state satisfies `Goal`. Thus

\[
d_{\mathrm{plan}}(X_3)\le 3.
\]

Combining the lower and upper bounds yields

\[
\boxed{d_{\mathrm{plan}}(X_3)=3}.
\]

The breadth-first audit returned 409 distinct shortest addition-set schedules, after expanding 89 planning nodes and evaluating 1,032 transitions. These counts are reproducible computational facts; the existence of many shortest schedules is not used in the exact-depth proof.

## 7. Consequence and claim boundary

The counterexample proves

\[
\boxed{\neg\bigl(\forall X\;[Goal\text{-reachable}(X)\Rightarrow d_{\mathrm{plan}}(X)\le2]\bigr)}.
\]

Equivalently, universal (k=2) is rejected under the frozen atomic semantics. The recovery mechanism of this representative is

\[
N1\text{-only}\ \longrightarrow\ N2\text{-only}\ \longrightarrow\ N2\text{-only}.
\]

The evidence does not yet establish any of the following:

- a universal finite depth bound (k\ge3);
- atomic completeness for all repairable states;
- atomic unreachability of any state;
- classification of all D6B3 admitted orbits;
- a theorem that the depth increase is caused specifically by overlapping N1 anchors.

The last boundary matters: the generator was motivated by interacting/overlap obstructions, but the exact certificate proves planning depth, not a unique geometric causal classification. In this representative, the added isolated component creates an additional N2 obligation, and both initial N1 choices destroy immediate viability of the same component-pair certificate. Calling this a certified “overlap mechanism” would require a separate structural analysis.

Under the frozen definition of (A_{N1}^{(1)}), an N1 action already removes all current local failures. Therefore an (N1\to N1\) schedule is not available in this action language; the realized depth-three mechanism is (N1\to N2\to N2).

## 8. ELI5 intuition

Imagine four islands that must become one, while a local crack must also be fixed. There are exactly two legal ways to fix the crack first. Whichever one is chosen, one bridge promise is temporarily lost. One new bridge can then join only part of the islands, so a second bridge is still necessary. No choice of just two legal moves completes both jobs, but three moves do: fix the crack, build one bridge, then build the final bridge.
