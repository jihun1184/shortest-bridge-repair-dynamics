# C07-D7 — Single-bridge absorption and satellite-extension audit

## 1. Verdict

C07-D7 separates three claims that were conflated after the exact-depth-three result.

1. For the D6C witness, the failure of every two-action plan has a finite structural explanation: after either valid N1 action, no shortest N2 bridge can absorb all four descendant components. This canonical-state result is **PROVED**.
2. Adding a second isolated satellite does not automatically increase planning depth from three to four. In the declared finite extension family, all 150 admitted states have exact depth two or three. The family is **EXHAUSTED with no depth-four hit**.
3. Recovery of the old N2 obligation and completion of the global goal are different horizons. At the D6C witness the old-obligation recovery horizon is one, while the residual goal depth after either N1 choice is two.

Consequently, the hypothesis that each added isolated component contributes one serial atomic action is not supported by this attack. The existence or nonexistence of a universal finite atomic-depth bound remains **OPEN**.

## 2. D7A: finite Single-Bridge Absorption Obstruction

Let the four components after an initial N1 repair be labelled

- `satellite`: the component containing \((-4,1,0)\);
- `left`: the component containing \((-3,-1,-1)\);
- `central`: the six-voxel descendant of the original central component;
- `right`: the component containing \((0,2,-1)\).

Both valid initial N1 actions preserve four components:

\[
4\xrightarrow{N1}4.
\]

For each of the six descendant-component pairs, the audit enumerated every endpoint pair attaining component distance \(d_6\), and then every monotone shortest ordering between those endpoints. This enumeration was performed before compatibility filtering. Thus it separates a geometric shortest-box limitation from a Q2/Q3 compatibility limitation.

### First N1 action \(\{(-1,-1,0)\}\)

| Component pair | \(d_6\) | Raw shortest orderings | Compatible orderings | Distinct actions | Maximum old components absorbed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| satellite–left | 4 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 2 |
| satellite–central | 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| satellite–right | 6 | 30 | 21 | 21 | 3 |
| left–central | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| left–right | 6 | 20 | 10 | 9 | 3 |
| central–right | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 |

Across 71 raw shortest orderings, zero absorb all four components. After compatibility and addition-set deduplication there are 46 N2 actions: 28 merge exactly two old components and 18 merge three. Their successor component counts are respectively three and two; none reaches `Goal`.

### First N1 action \(\{(0,0,0)\}\)

| Component pair | \(d_6\) | Raw shortest orderings | Compatible orderings | Distinct actions | Maximum old components absorbed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| satellite–left | 4 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 2 |
| satellite–central | 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 2 |
| satellite–right | 6 | 30 | 18 | 18 | 3 |
| left–central | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| left–right | 6 | 20 | 7 | 6 | 3 |
| central–right | 3 | 7 | 5 | 3 | 2 |

Across 74 raw shortest orderings, again zero absorb all four components. The 40 distinct compatible N2 actions comprise 28 two-component absorptions and 12 three-component absorptions. None reaches `Goal`.

Therefore, for this canonical state,

\[
\boxed{
\forall \Delta_1\in A_{N1}^{(1)}(X_3),\quad
\forall \Delta_2\in A_{N2}(X_3\cup\Delta_1),\quad
|\pi_0^{(6)}(X_3\cup\Delta_1\cup\Delta_2)|\ge2.
}
\]

The key point is that full absorption is absent even among incompatible raw shortest orderings. The obstruction is therefore caused by shortest-box geometry in this fixture, rather than by compatibility masks deleting otherwise fully absorbing bridges.

This is a finite canonical-state lemma, not yet a state-independent symbolic theorem.

## 3. D7B: second-satellite extension census

The D7B generator adds one voxel \(s_2\) to the D6C witness \(X_3\), requiring it to be a new isolated 6-component. Candidate coordinates lie in the bounding box of \(X_3\) expanded by three units, with

\[
2\le d_6(s_2,X_3)\le4.
\]

States were quotiented by translations and signed-axis permutations. Planner admission further required

\[
has\_fail(X)=True,qquad A_{N1}^{(1)}(X)\ne\varnothing,
\]

\[
A_{N1}^{(1)}(X)\cap A_{N2}(X)=\varnothing,
\]

and no action in \(A_{\mathrm{atom}}(X)\) satisfying `P_preserve`.

The exact-depth census is

| Atomic planning depth | States |

The search used state-deduplicated breadth-first traversal. This is exact for reachability depth because the frozen action semantics are Markovian: the available actions and `Goal` depend only on the current voxel set, not on the schedule used to reach it. As an implementation audit, all 19 depth-two states and six depth-three shell-boundary representatives were independently rerun with the full addition-sequence planner; all 25 depths matched.

The presence of 19 depth-two extensions is especially informative. An additional isolated component can create a shortest bridge that absorbs several previously separate components at once, thereby reducing rather than increasing the planning depth of the depth-three core. Component count alone is therefore neither an exact depth measure nor a monotone lower bound under this action model.

The result is restricted to the declared coordinate and distance family. It does not refute the existence of a differently placed depth-four satellite extension.

## 4. Recovery horizon versus residual goal depth

For an original state \(X\) and a fixed initial N1 action \(\Delta\), define the old-obligation recovery horizon

\[
r_{\mathrm{old}}(X,\Delta)
=
\min\left\{h:\begin{array}{l}
\text{after an atomic continuation of length }h,\\
\text{every certificate in }C_{N2}(X)\text{ is viable}\\
\text{between its current descendants}
\end{array}\right\}.
\]

Define separately the residual goal depth

\[
g_{\mathrm{res}}(X,\Delta)
=\min\{h:Goal\text{ is reached after }\Delta\text{ and }h\text{ further actions}\}.
\]

For both initial N1 choices at \(X_3\):

| Initial N1 | Old certificates viable immediately | One-action continuations restoring all old certificates | \(r_{\mathrm{old}}\) | \(g_{\mathrm{res}}\) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(\{(-1,-1,0)\}\) | 5/6 | 43/46 | 1 | 2 |
| \(\{(0,0,0)\}\) | 5/6 | 37/40 | 1 | 2 |

In the explicit D6C plan, the first N2 action already restores viability of all six original certificates, but the resulting state is not yet `Goal`. The final N2 action is required for connectivity completion.

Hence D6C does **not** show that recovery of the lost old obligation requires two-step look-ahead. It shows that, after a locally necessary N1 repair, one action can recover the old obligations while two subsequent actions are required to complete the global goal.

## 5. Structural interpretation and next hypothesis

The data support an absorption-based view of atomic planning:

\[
\text{depth is controlled by the sequence of component partitions induced by admissible bridges,}
\]

not by raw component count alone. A useful next object is the **absorption rank** of an action: the number and identity of current components mapped to one descendant by that action. A depth-growth construction must prevent later bridges from jointly absorbing several satellites; spatial separation alone is insufficient.

A sharper D7C attack would therefore construct a family of component sets whose admissible shortest boxes form an absorption antichain, or prove that such an antichain cannot persist under the present N2 action semantics. Only such a result could support or refute an unbounded-depth theorem without confusing planning interaction with ordinary connectivity burden.

## 6. ELI5 intuition

Think of islands and bridges. The depth-three example has four islands after fixing a crack. Every shortest legal bridge can join at most three of them, so one bridge can never finish the job. But adding a fifth island does not necessarily require another bridge: if the new island lies along a useful route, one bridge may collect several islands together like beads on a string. What matters is not how many islands exist, but which groups one legal bridge can collect at once.
