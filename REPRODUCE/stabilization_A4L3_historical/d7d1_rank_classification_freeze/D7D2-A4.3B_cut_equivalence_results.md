# D7D2-A4.3B — Cut-equivalence template extraction

> **Subsequent status:** the finite A4-L1a audit certified the fixed
> remainder, and A4-L1b proved the resulting formal all-k geometry using
> the three corridor laws. Reachability and behavioral membership for
> k>8 remain open in A4-L2.

## Question

The A4.3 signature was inconsistent for 24 of the 53 exact behavioral
classes across `k=6,7,8`.  The smallest example showed that the extractor
forced the thin corridor to lie on the satellite's own `(y,z)` line even
when the component immediately bent onto another line.  The question was
whether the 24 mismatches reflected different geometry or only different
tail/patch cut conventions.

## Revised intrinsic comparison

The revised extractor makes three changes.

1. The whole satellite-side initial x-slice is eligible as a fixed elbow
   attachment; a straight corridor may begin at any point of that slice.
2. A multi-point slice containing the next point of the straight line is
   included as the corridor endpoint.  It is not cut one slice too early.
3. When more than one corridor cut is admissible, the representative is
   assigned the set of admissible signatures.  Corresponding
   representatives are consistent when these sets have a nonempty
   intersection.  This explicitly quotients out cut convention instead
   of selecting an arbitrary longest-run convention.

Each individual signature records the corridor-line offset from the
satellite, the satellite-side attachment shape, and the core-side patch
shape anchored at the corridor endpoint.  Corridor length is omitted.

## Exact finite result

The audit rebuilt the three full closures and exact behavioral keys from
source, selected the canonical minimum-size representative of every key,
and compared the revised cut-equivalence signatures.

```text
k=6:  9,524 states, 53 classes
k=7: 21,963 states, 53 classes
k=8: 45,939 states, 53 classes

common classes:                53
cut-consistent classes:        53
cut-inconsistent classes:       0
distinct witnessed signatures: 18
```

The previously smallest inconsistent class now has the same witnessed
cut at all three k values: corridor offset `(0,-1,0)`, the same two-voxel
satellite attachment, and an empty core-side patch.

The intermediate unique-longest-cut convention improved the old result
from 29/53 to 52/53.  Its sole residual mismatch was a boundary
degeneration: at `k=6` a satellite-side corridor has length zero and its
elbow shares the same x-slice, while at `k=7,8` that corridor has positive
length.  The set-valued cut-equivalence formulation resolves this without
asserting that one of the two geometrically valid cuts is intrinsically
preferred.

## Claim boundary

This closes the diagnosed A4.3 signature defect for the three computed
closures.  It establishes a finite `53/53` representative-template
consistency result for `k=6,7,8`.

It does **not** prove the Parametric Representative Lemma for every
`k>=6`, does not yet prove uniform labeled-transition preservation, and
does not prove closure exhaustiveness.  Thus `Q_k ~= Q_6` for all `k>=6`
and `R*(X_k) <= 3` for all `k>=6` remain open.
